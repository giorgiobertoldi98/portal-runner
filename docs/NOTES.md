# Portal Runner Notes

> Living reference for the game. Always read before working on the project. Update it whenever something changes, and keep it a description of the current state only, with no history or references to past implementations.

## Concept
Portal Runner is a personal 3D game built with Three.js, played in the browser. It has polished graphics and sound. It must be fun but not impossible.

## Design principles
General principles that guide every decision. New ones are added here as they come up.
- **Show, don't tell.** When the game wants you to do something (open a new item, take something, notice a tool), it prompts visually (a glow, a pulse, the item's name beside it) rather than with a toast. Toasts are for what can't be shown. Whenever it prompts you to open or use something, the prompt carries an arrow pointing at it and one or two plain words (the item's name, "Take the note").
- **Let the player work it out.** Hints and toasts suggest rather than explain: short, a little cryptic, often in the explorer's own voice ("This gem is slowing me down..."). It's fine if a player doesn't get it straight away: figuring it out is part of the fun.
- **Don't spoil.** A world's arrival line (shown as you start) gives only its atmosphere, in a few words and no verbs: never what is there or what hunts you.
- **Keep the HUD quiet.** Few toasts, small and out of the way, in the HUD's own style; nothing covers the middle of the screen unless you asked for it (a held item).
- **What you find stays with you in that world.** Items gather as you find them and stay until you leave the world through its portal; being caught doesn't take them away.

## Screens and orientation
- **Landscape only**, on mobile and on PC. Every screen (menu, Settings, the run, the note, the map, pause and end screens) is designed for a screen wider than it is tall.
- On a screen taller than it is wide (a phone held upright, or a PC window narrower than it is tall) a full-screen notice covers everything: a phone icon turning sideways, "Portal Runner plays sideways" and, on touch, "Turn your device to landscape. If it doesn't rotate, switch off rotation lock" (on PC: "Make the window wider than it is tall"). Nothing is drawn behind it. A run in progress pauses, so turning the phone back shows the pause screen and nothing happens until you press Resume.
- Browsers don't let a page lock the orientation (outside fullscreen), which is why the game blocks portrait with the notice instead of rotating itself.
- **Every screen must look right on small and large screens alike.** Build flexibly from the start (relative units, no fixed assumptions). While developing, check two sizes: a standard PC (1280×720) and a standard phone held sideways (667×375, touch emulated). Wider coverage (a large phone, a tablet, a large monitor, a near-square window) is a pass for the end of development. Nothing may overlap, be cut off or make a screen scroll where it shouldn't, and controls must stay comfortable to reach on touch.

## Structure
- The game is a sequence of levels. Each level is a themed world.
- World order: **Glacier → Alien → Desert**.
- Each world is its own module, so adding a new world means adding a new file rather than changing the others.
- Each world can bring its own hunter behaviour ("brain"), its own mechanics (e.g. the glacier's snow storms and ice hole) and its own settings. A world without a brain gets the plain chase hunter.
- One calibrated game mode. Default rules: 3 lives; 3 gems needed in each of the three worlds. Each world holds exactly the number of gems it needs: no spares. All values below are defaults and can be changed in Settings.

## Main menu (landing view)
- A live 3D scene of the void between worlds: a winding chain of floating portals climbs into the distance, one per world in the registry, each swirling with its world's colours and with no name on it. The nearest is clear; the farther ones fade into haze, so you only glimpse what's coming. Dark gems drift past and glint now and then.
- It names no specific world: the copy is about the expedition as a whole. The title is filled with the worlds' colours, drifting slowly (still when reduced motion is on).
- Layout: the text sits on the left, vertically centred, over a dark wash that fades out towards the middle of the screen; the portals are on the right. The text block is at most 440 px wide and never more than 56% of the screen width, so the portals always keep the right side. On screens up to 640 px tall the three-line summary is hidden and the title gets smaller; up to 480 px tall (phones held sideways) the title follows the screen height and the spacing tightens.
- **The menu never scrolls**: everything fits on one screen.
- **Legibility**: the text never sits on top of the portals. The game measures where the text ends and fits the portals (their frames and decorations) into the space to its right, sliding the view away from the text and shrinking it slightly if needed. The lead, summary and controls lines have a soft dark text shadow; the title has a dark shadow under its glow.
- Buttons: **Begin the expedition** and **Settings**. Below the intro, a three-line summary of the core loop: hunt the dark gems hidden in every world, carry them to the portal (each one slows you down), fill the portal and step through to the next world. The copy doesn't describe how gems are found. Below the buttons, the controls for the device in use (see Controls; including Space to take things, and the wheel and Space to pick and read what you find).
- **Sound**: one calm background track shared by the main menu and Settings, belonging to no single world: a slow C-major pad, a low drone, a rare glass note and a faint airy hush. Browsers allow sound only after a gesture, so it fades in on the first touch, click or key at the menu. A run plays at a lower overall level (see Technical rules) so the game doesn't jump out at you after the menu. Returning to the menu from a run crossfades back to the menu track, raises the level back and silences all in-game voices (gem hum, portal hum, heartbeat).
- Everything is built from the world registry, so a new world appears in the landing view automatically.

## Settings
- Opened from the main menu (**Settings** button). Changes apply from the next run and are remembered in the browser. **Reset to defaults** restores the values in this document.
- **General**: footstep sounds on/off (default on), base speed V (12 m/s), speed lost per gem (11% of V per gem carried, 0–30%; never below 45% of V), portal safe radius (13 m), lives (3).
- Hints that quote your carrying speeds (Yeti approach speed, storm close chase) update to match the speed lost per gem.
- **Worlds** (one tab per world, named after it, no numbers):
  - Every world: gems needed (3, range 1–12). Exactly this many gems are hidden in the world.
  - Glacier: Yeti approach distance (45 m), Yeti approach speed (65% of V), Yeti storm sprint (260% of V, 100–400%; its speed in a storm while farther than its approach distance), Yeti storm close chase (115% of V, 40–200%; its speed in a storm within its approach distance), Yeti forget time (8 s), number of storm devices (10), device trigger radius (15 m), storm radius min and max (45 m and 80 m; each storm picks a random radius in that range; min and max push each other so min ≤ max), ice hole distance (110 m, 30–220; the hole with the explorers' map never opens closer than this to the portal or to the spawn point).
- Adding a setting: general ones go in `src/settings.js`; per-world ones go in the world module's `settings` list.

## Controls
- **Touch**: the left thumb moves with a joystick (it jumps to where the thumb lands anywhere on the left side); directions are relative to the camera, the explorer turns to face the way it walks, and pushing to the edge runs. The right side of the screen moves the camera (drag sideways to look around, up and down to tilt).
- **PC**: WASD or the arrow keys move (relative to the camera), Shift runs, the mouse moves the camera (drag, or move once the pointer is locked by a click). Q drops a gem, Space takes things when something is within reach and otherwise holds up the selected item, the mouse wheel (or 1–9) picks one of your items and Space holds it up (Space again puts it away; it takes things first when something is within reach), Esc / P pause, M mute. On touch, tapping an item's button holds it up.
- **Holding an item up** (the map, the note; see Held items): each item decides which controls still work. The map: camera only (you can't walk or run), and pinch (two fingers anywhere), the mouse wheel or + / − zoom it. The note: neither walking nor the camera.
- **With the map open**, turning the camera (mouse, arrow keys, right side of the screen) also turns the explorer to face where the camera looks (it eases round and keeps going until aligned), so the map's arrow turns with it and you can point yourself. With the map closed the camera never turns the explorer. Nothing tells you this: you find out by turning.
- Standing still (letting go of the joystick, or no movement key held) is what sends the Resonance Call in worlds that allow it.

## HUD and camera
- The game never shows level numbers to the player: the HUD, the arrival banner and the end screen name the world only (e.g. "The Frozen Peaks"). The end screen shows time, gems delivered and catches, plus the world you were caught in (on a loss) or the lives left (on a win).
- **Top left**: one compact chip, the same design on every screen (a little larger on screens at least 900 px wide and 560 px tall): the world name, then the gem icon (a stylised silhouette of that world's gem, in its colour) with "0 / 3 in the portal" (the label always stays, so it is clear the count is gems already inserted) and a "+n carried" badge beside the count while you carry gems, in the colour of that world's gems (blue, pink, amber). The portal opening is shown by the chip's glowing border (no toast).
- **Top right**: hearts, sound and pause only (system buttons, not game tools).
- **Toasts**: one short line in a small chip in the HUD's style (translucent panel, thin border, a dot in the world's colour), centred just below the top row so it never meets the world chip, however wide that grows. It fades in with a slight drop. While an item is held up it sits on the item's top edge. The full list is under Toasts below.
- **Tools group**: everything you can do with what you hold or what's within reach is grouped together: your items (see Items), Drop gem (while carrying; in the colour of that world's gems, and on touch it shows a count of carried gems in the same colour) and the contextual action (e.g. "Take the map" next to the crate), which glows and pulses so it's noticed. On PC it sits at the bottom centre: the items' slot bar, then Drop gem as a labelled pill showing its key (Q). The action ("Take the note") is a pulsing pill in the upper half of the screen, centred, showing Space. On touch it's in the right-hand gutter above the look pad: the action at the bottom (nearest the thumb) as a pill that always shows its label, Drop gem above it, then the items; tools show their name beside them for a few seconds the first time they appear, and items keep it while they call for attention.
- **Items** (things you hold up to read: the note, the map, future documents): they gather in the order you find them and stay until you leave the world through its portal (being caught doesn't take them). A newly found item is selected and glows with a slow, repeating pulse until you first hold it up; that glow, with the item's name and an arrow pointing at it, is the only prompt. On PC they form a slot bar of icon buttons (the size and look of the system buttons, with their number 1–9 in the corner); the selected slot is ringed in the world's colour and the others are dimmed. Just below the bar, a quiet key guide is always shown (PC only): the mouse wheel (mouse players) or 1–9 (keyboard only) to pick, shown only once there is more than one item, and Space to open, on one line as plain text with a soft dark shadow, no container ("[wheel] pick · [Space] open"). A small label above the bar names the selected item and, when there is more than one, a mouse-wheel icon. It shows for a moment after the selection changes, and stays while the selected item calls for attention. The mouse wheel or 1–9 picks an item, Space holds it up, and clicking a slot holds it up directly. On touch each item is a button: a tap holds it up. They fill a column of up to 4 (2 on screens up to 500 px tall), then wrap into new columns to the left, so they never climb into the sound / pause buttons.
- **Touch layout**: the right-hand elements share one gutter (14 px from the edge, plus the safe area; 10 px on screens up to 500 px tall): the sound / pause buttons at the top, the tools column and the look pad at the bottom all line up on it. Tool buttons have the same size and shape as the sound / pause buttons (40 px rounded squares; 36 px on screens up to 500 px tall), with the same translucent panel, so they read as part of the HUD without covering the world.
- **Touch pads**: compact, without labels, low in the bottom corners. On taller screens (tablets): joystick 124 px, look pad 88 px. On screens up to 500 px tall (phones held sideways): joystick 112 px and look pad 84 px; hearts and system buttons shrink; the Resonance Call chip moves to the bottom centre. The joystick's reach follows its size.
- **Camera**: a third-person orbit (62° field of view, 7.5 m behind the explorer). Dragging (mouse, or the right side of a touch screen) turns it; dragging up looks up. While you walk without touching the camera for over a second, it swings gently behind the explorer, except while you walk backward (S or pulling the stick back): then the explorer turns and walks straight towards the camera instead of circling. The camera itself never swings much below the explorer's head height (it would dig into the ground): past that point the gaze tilts upward instead, up to about 24°, keeping the explorer in the lower part of the view (`rig.lookLift` in `src/engine/cameraRig.js`; the ice cave applies the same tilt, scaled to its shorter camera distance).

## Held items (the map, the note)
- Things you hold up to read share one behaviour. They open **almost full screen**, inset by a thin border (`--held-m`: 2.8% of the shorter screen side, 10–28 px, plus safe areas) where the world stays visible, so their proportions always follow the screen's. Each fills its own shape with its own background: the map's square chart sits on an unrolled scroll, the note's sketch on a handwritten page.
- While one is open, the HUD steps aside (world chip, hearts, sound / pause, tools, Resonance Call chip and arrival banner are hidden). On PC the pointer is released so the cursor can reach the controls.
- **Each item has its own settings** (`hold` in its module): which pads still work (`pads`: 'both' = walk and turn, 'right' = turn the camera only, 'none' = neither; a pad that doesn't work is hidden, and on PC the movement keys / mouse follow the same rule), whether it zooms (`zoom`), and whether the game pauses while it's open (`pauses`).
  - **Map**: pads 'right' (turn the camera only), zoom yes, the game keeps running.
  - **Note**: pads 'none', no zoom, the game keeps running.
- Controls in the top-right corner: a round close button, then, on items that zoom, zoom in (+) and zoom out (−), which grey out at their limits. On those items, pinching with two fingers anywhere, the mouse wheel and the + / − keys zoom too.
- Closing: the close button on both, or Space; the map also with Esc; the note also with Esc / Enter / Space. Opening one item puts the other away; being caught or leaving the world puts both away (they stay among your items).

## Toasts
Every toast in the game (see Design principles: anything that can be shown instead isn't a toast):
- **"Let go of the stick to send a Resonance Call"** (touch) / **"Stand still to send a Resonance Call"** (PC): once per run, 3.8 s after the first world that allows the call opens (the Alien world in a full run). A world's own `introHint` replaces it; a world where the call is silent (the Glacier) skips it.
- **"This gem is slowing me down..."**: when you pick up your first gem in the first world, before any delivery.
- **"<Hunter> caught you — n lives left · m gems dropped"**: after a catch with lives left; the gems part only if you were carrying some (plus a world's `onRespawn` note, if it has one).
- **"Winds are changing…"** (Glacier): whenever the storm devices shift (taking the map; a gem going into the portal, at most once every 10 s, never the gem that opens it).
- **"Snow storms... make the Yeti so hungry!"** (Glacier): the first storm each time the Glacier is set up.

## Scale
- The player is 1.8 m tall.
- The glacier's playable area is a disc ~258 m in radius (~515 m across); about 70% of it is walkable (the rest is lake or steep slopes). Running across it takes about 45 seconds.

## The explorer (player character)
- A low-poly futuristic explorer, built in `src/entities/player.js`. Palette: slate-blue undersuit, warm-white armour plates (helmet, cuirass, pauldrons, forearm guards, greaves, boots), orange trims (helmet crest, pauldron rings, chest tag, canister caps, boot toes) and cyan suit lights. The dark undersuit and orange trims keep the figure readable against snow, sand and the alien ground alike.
- Helmet with a dark glass wraparound visor (glowing cyan rim) and ear pods; the face is never shown.
- The back, which the game camera sees most, carries the tech pack: a glowing light column, two side canisters with thrusters, a whip antenna that sways with movement, and a gem emitter on top that the carried gems orbit above. A wrist scanner sits on the left forearm.
- Suit lights (visor rim, chest, belt, pack, boot soles) glow steadily while moving and brighten with a slow pulse while you stand still, matching the moment you search for gems.
- Each bone of the rig holds one merged mesh per material (suit, plates, visor, lights), so the figure costs about 30 draw calls. The rig (hips, torso, head, arms, legs, antenna) is what the walk/run animation moves.

## Speeds
Every speed is a fixed fraction of the player's running speed **V = 12 m/s**. Changing V rescales the whole game. Hunter speeds depend only on the hunter's state, never on time spent in the world.

| Who / state | Speed | m/s |
|---|---|---|
| Player walking | 0.5 V | 6 |
| Player running | V | 12 |
| Player running with n gems | (1 − 0.11·n) V, never below 0.45 V (0.11 = speed lost per gem, editable) | 10.7 / 9.4 / 8.0 with 1 / 2 / 3 gems |
| Yeti searching | 0.4 V | 4.8 |
| Yeti approaching | 0.65 V | 7.8 |
| Yeti in a storm, farther than its approach distance (storm sprint, editable) | 2.6 V | 31.2 |
| Yeti in a storm, within its approach distance (storm close chase, editable) | 1.15 V | 13.8 |
| Plain-chase hunters (Alien, Desert) | 0.65 V | 7.8 |
| Plain-chase hunters while you're in the portal safe zone (moving away) | 0.4 V | 4.8 |

Why these values:
- **Tension comes from what you carry, not from a timer.** The decision that matters is how many gems to risk carrying at once, so each hunter speed sits at or below a carrying speed rather than rising over time.
- **Searching (0.4 V)** is slower than walking: a wandering Yeti is scenery, never a threat on its own.
- **Approaching (0.65 V)** is just below your running speed even with a full load of 3 gems (0.67 V), so you can always outrun it in the open, but with all 3 gems only barely: a 2% margin that any turn or obstacle eats up. The danger is otherwise a short approach distance (45 m, under 4 seconds of running) and the storms, not raw speed.
- **Storm sprint (2.6 V)** is how storms turn a distant Yeti into an immediate one. From 250 m away it reaches the edge of its approach distance in about 7 seconds; running out of a storm from its centre takes about 4–7 s carrying nothing and 6–10 s carrying 3 gems, so a Yeti that starts far away usually still catches you if you carry gems. Lowering the sprint makes far-away storms safer.
- **Storm close chase (1.15 V)** is faster than your gem-free running speed, so once the Yeti is within its approach distance in a storm you can't outrun it: you must reach the storm's edge or the portal safe zone before it closes in, and carrying gems only makes that harder. It switches abruptly from the sprint to this speed at the Yeti's approach distance (the same setting decides both). The sprint only happens beyond that distance, which is deep inside the storm's fog, so you rarely see it run at sprint speed.
- **Plain chase (0.65 V)** is gentler because those hunters always know where you are. You outrun them carrying up to 3 gems, only just with all 3.

## Core loop (per world)
1. Explore the world looking for **dark gems**. The world holds exactly as many as the portal needs, so every gem counts.
2. Pick up gems and carry them to the portal.
3. Insert the gems into the portal to activate it. Gems go in one every 0.35 s while you're within 7.5 m of the portal.
4. The portal to the next world appears only once the current world is completed.

## Gems
- Dark gems are hidden. In most worlds they are found by **standing still** (the Resonance Call): a ring of light illuminates nearby gems, and a trail points toward the closest one. A world can silence the call: in the Glacier it never works, and the explorers' map shows the gems instead (see below).
- **Proximity hum**: a gem on the ground hums and glimmers faintly as you come near, louder and brighter as you close in. How near depends on whether you have a way to find gems in that world. With one (a world where the call works, or once you hold the explorers' map in the Glacier) it starts within 14 m. Without one (the Glacier before the map) it starts only within 4 m, barely beyond the 1.9 m pickup reach, so you can't find gems by wandering around; the cue only confirms a gem you're practically touching.
- The first hint explaining how to send the call appears the first time the call is available in a run (at the start of the first world that doesn't silence it: the Alien world in a full run). On touch it reads "Let go of the stick to send a Resonance Call".
- Gems can be carried, at the speed cost above. A carried gem can be dropped (Q, or the Drop gem tool) and picked up again. It is set down about 1 m behind you.
- If the hunter catches you, the gems you were carrying drop around where you were caught, about 2.5 m apart. Every dropped gem lands on ground you can walk back to: never inside a prop, off the map, or past a height step of more than 1 m (into the ice hole, onto a cliff); if no spot nearby qualifies, it lands where you stood. Gems already in the portal stay there. Since there are no spares, a dropped gem must be fetched again. Dropped gems keep a faint glow.
- **Look**: every world has its own gem: a solid (not transparent), rough, faceted crystal about 1.6 m across its longest side, floating and turning slowly. Hidden it shows a dark tone; close by (the proximity hum) it glimmers and turns its colour, so in the Glacier a gem lights up only when you're right next to it until you hold the map; revealed by the call it turns its full colour, glows and sends up a plume of particles in its glow colour. A dropped gem keeps most of its colour and a faint glow.
  - **Glacier: glacier cluster**: a fist of hexagonal ice prisms growing from a frozen lump; dark grey hidden, clear mid blue revealed.
  - **Alien: geode**: a rough, round stone of dark rock, pierced by 14 small holes spread unevenly all around it, through which a pink crystal lining shows from any angle; only the lining glows and turns pink (the rock just catches the light). Hidden, the lining is dark too, so the holes barely show.
  - **Desert: desert rose**: a bloom of rounded crystal petals, a tight bud opening into wider, outward-leaning rings; brown hidden, amber revealed (softer glow so the petals keep their shading).
  - The gems you carry (orbiting the backpack) and the ones in the portal sockets are small versions of the same shape, in the world's revealed colour (the geode's is its pink core). An empty socket is a hole in the shape of that gem, near-black with a thin pale rim, filled by the gem when it goes in. The menu's drifting gems mix all three designs.

## Enemies
- Each world has one hunter, and each hunter's behaviour is custom to its world.
- Hunters aim slightly ahead of where you're running rather than at where you are.
- **Portal safe zone (every world)**: within 13 m of the portal (beyond the 7.5 m insertion range, so you can always feed gems in safely), no hunter can follow you, whatever its distance or state, storms included. While you're inside, it stops chasing, can't enter the zone and can't reach you from its edge, and the danger cues are off. Once you step out, its normal behaviour resumes straight away. Each hunter decides what it does meanwhile (the Yeti goes back to searching, heading away from you; plain-chase hunters walk away from the portal). A radius of 0 turns the safe zone off.
- You start each level (and respawn after being caught) facing the middle of the world, just inside the edge of the safe zone: 1.5 m in from the ring (11.5 m from the portal by default), never on the portal platform. If that spot isn't walkable, the start swings around the portal at the same distance, then a little closer.
- The zone is marked by a faint ring on the ground in the portal's glow colour. It follows the terrain, breathes gently and fades out when the portal opens.
- The hunter vanishes once the portal is activated (last needed gem inserted).
- Danger cues (red screen-edge vignette and heartbeat) tell you the hunter is close. Each hunter defines what "close" means; by default it is within 55 m, getting stronger as it nears.

### Glacier — the Yeti
- **Search** (default): wanders between random waypoints. It doesn't know where you are.
- **Approach**: when you come within 45 m, it heads for you.
- **Giving up**: once you're outside its approach distance (45 m) again, it gives up, turns toward a waypoint well away from you and won't approach again for 8 s (a storm still brings it). Whenever it gives up on you (here, or when you reach the portal safe zone or the ice hole), the waypoint is chosen to lead away from you, never past or around you, so it never drifts closer first.
- **In the portal safe zone**: it goes back to searching, heading for a waypoint well away from you. Once you step out, the normal rules apply straight away (no forget time): if it's within 45 m, it approaches.
- **In the ice hole** (the pit, its ledge or the cave): it can't come in (the ledge and tunnel are too narrow for it) and loses track of you while you're inside, exactly as in the portal safe zone. Any snow storm dies the moment you enter, and none can start while you're down there, so it has nothing to find you by when you climb out. It can still catch you standing on the rim outside.
- **Storm chase**: while you're outside the portal safe zone and inside a snow storm, it knows where you are: it sprints (storm sprint speed) while farther than its approach distance (45 m), then switches abruptly to its close-chase speed. The approach distance setting moves this switch point too.
- **When you leave a storm**: it loses track of you. If you're within 45 m it approaches; otherwise it searches, walking toward a random waypoint.
- Danger cues are on exactly while it is hunting you (never inside the portal safe zone or the ice hole): approaching, or within 45 m in a storm. They start at a clear level and grow stronger as it gets closer, and switch off the moment it gives up, so you know you got away.

### Alien and Desert hunters
- Plain chase: always know where you are and head for you; in the portal safe zone they walk away from the portal. Custom behaviours to be designed.

## Glacier — the ice hole and the explorers' map
- **The Resonance Call is silent in the Glacier**: standing still never sends a ring or a trail here. Nothing says so: you find out by standing still. Until you hold the map, gems only hum from within 4 m (see Gems), so the map is effectively the way to find them.
- **The note**: a handwritten page lying in the snow a few steps from where you start (about 4.5 m ahead, a little to one side): a sheet of amber paper that stands out against the snow, floating about a metre up, bobbing and turning slowly, with a glow around it and no shadow. Within reach a glowing "Take the note" action appears, with an arrow; taking it (Space on PC, or tap the action) makes the note your first item (selected, glowing with a slow, repeating pulse and an arrow pointing at it until you open it; on touch its name shows beside it; on PC the label above the slot bar names it). No toast. It never opens by itself and, like the map, stays among your items for the whole world. The page shows a quick hand-drawn sketch (ink and blue wash) of a ring of blue ice spikes standing in the snow, seen from almost ground level, with four spires a little above the rest, and mountains and pines behind; and one handwritten line: "Thought I saw ice spikes in a circle somewhere...". No title, no figure, nothing else. It deliberately gives nothing away: no opening, no way in, no map, no hole: only what the spikes look like, never where they are or what they are. It is a held item (see Held items: no pads, no zoom, the game keeps running). On screens at least 1.5 times wider than tall the sketch (pasted on the page, slightly tilted) fills the left and the line sits on the right, centred beside it; on squarer screens the sketch is on top and the line below it.
- **The hole**: a sinkhole 14 m across and 7.5 m deep, ringed by a crown of blue-ice shards leaning outward, with a faint blue glow rising from inside. Four spires (3.1–3.8 m) stand a little above the rest of the crown (shards 1.2–3.5 m). It is kept low on purpose, so the hole doesn't give itself away from across the map. One wide gap in the shards, marked by an old explorers' marker pole (red tip, blue cloth), is the only way in. Behind it, a flat landing at ground level (about 3.5 m along the rim, a path out about 3.4 m wide) tops a ledge about 3 m wide that spirals down the wall (about 300° in all) to the floor, so you can step in or out anywhere along the landing; walking down takes a few seconds. Every other edge is a sheer wall you can't step off or climb, so you leave the way you came.
- **Spotting it**: the low crown shows only from fairly close (it hides behind small ridges and drifts). Within 50 m (outside the cave), a hollow wind moans out of the hole every 3–5.5 s, louder as you get closer.
- **The cave**: at the bottom, a vaulted tunnel (about 4.3 m high at its centre) leads under the ground into a domed chamber (about 5.7 m high at its centre, 3.3 m at its edge): an abandoned explorers' camp (crates with a lit lantern, bedroll, backpack, rope, pickaxe), icicles and glowing ice crystals, water dripping now and then. The map lies rolled up on a crate, glowing warmly. Above the cave, a mound of snowy rock marks ground you can't walk on.
- **Camera in the hole**: it lowers and comes closer gradually as you walk from the shaft floor towards the tunnel mouth, so it is already low when the vault closes over you, with no sudden jump.
- **Where it opens**: 12 clearings are prepared when the world is built (gentle ground, spread out, away from the landmarks, the lake and the portals, kept free of props). Each run picks one at random at least the ice hole distance (110 m) from the portal and from the spawn point, with a random orientation. Gems, Yeti waypoints and storm devices never use any of the clearings, and devices stay at least their trigger radius + 8 m beyond the hole and the cave.
- **The map**: within about 2 m of the crate, a glowing "Take the map" action appears in the tools group. The map is taken only when you use it (Space on PC, or tap the action): rustle and chime, and the map joins your items (selected, glowing until you first open it; no toast). Taking it also makes the storm devices shift (see Glacier snow storms); their chime and "Winds are changing…" toast come after the map's own. The map opens **only by hand**: Space while it's selected, or its button among your items; it closes with its close button, Space or Esc. It is a held item (see Held items: turn pad only, zoom, the game keeps running). It is an unrolled scroll filling the screen: wooden rods with knobs at both ends, parchment between them, and in the middle a square hand-drawn chart of the Frozen Peaks (contour lines, lake, cliffs, trees, landmarks: Camp, Watchtower, Mammoth, Ice arches, Frozen lake, Frozen falls) as tall as the scroll allows, keeping at least 118 px (150 px on screens 900 px wide or more) of parchment on each side. On the left parchment: the world's name and the legend (You, Gems, Portal, Ice hole; each with the same little symbol the chart draws, the hole as the same spiral); on the right: a compass; in the top-right corner, the close and zoom controls. On the chart: you as an orange arrow pointing the way the explorer faces (plus a faint view cone; with the map open, turning the camera turns the explorer, see Controls), the portal as a blue ring with a soft pulsing halo, the ice hole as a blue spiral labelled "Ice hole", and every gem lying on the ground as a pulsing diamond with a glow, in the colour of that world's gems (the part that glows when revealed). Marks keep the same size on screen whatever the zoom (a little larger than the chart's own drawing scale: `MARK` = 1.15 CSS px per unit). Gems lying closer together than a diamond's width on the chart (e.g. the ones you lose when caught) share one diamond with a count badge in the same colour, so a pile never reads as a single gem.
- **Zoom** (1× to 4×): at 1× the whole world shows; zoomed in, the chart centres on you, stopping at the map's edge, and its lines, trees and labels are redrawn sharp for the new scale. Anything out of view (gems, the portal, the hole) gets a small pointer in its colour at the chart's edge, pointing towards it. The zoom level is kept between openings.
- **It can't be dropped or lost.** If the Yeti catches you, it stays in your hands when you come back.
- The map belongs to one run of the Glacier; other worlds have no map.

## Glacier snow storms
- 10 hidden storm devices, placed at the start of each run. They are spread evenly over the walkable map (best-candidate sampling, which also keeps them off the border) and stay at least 45 m from the portal and 55 m from the spawn point, and clear of the ice hole.
- No gem ever starts inside a trigger zone: devices are always placed at least the trigger radius + 6 m (21 m by default) from every gem on the ground, so fetching a gem never forces a storm.
- **Devices shift** (all together, to new spots following the same placement rules and also staying at least 70 m from you) on two occasions: when you take the explorers' map, and when a gem goes into the portal. After a shift, further insertions don't shift them again for 10 s, so feeding several gems at once causes one shift. Every shift is marked by a falling glassy chime and the toast "Winds are changing…". The gem that opens the portal doesn't shift them, since devices stop triggering once the portal is open.
- Devices are invisible. The only cue is a hum that starts 6 m before a device's 15 m trigger radius and swells until you cross it.
- Crossing a device's trigger radius starts a storm: a fixed circle centred where you stood, with a random radius of 45–80 m.
- Inside a storm: thick fog and a blizzard cut visibility to ~30 m (never zero). A glowing line on the ground and a snow curtain mark its edge. A gust, a roar from the Yeti and wind sound announce it; the first storm of a run also brings the toast "Snow storms... make the Yeti so hungry!".
- Only one storm at a time. Devices don't trigger once the portal is open, nor for 4 seconds after a storm ends.
- Leaving the circle ends the storm, and so does going down into the ice hole. The device stays where it is: walking back into its trigger radius starts a new storm. Between shifts you can learn where devices are; taking the map and each delivery reshuffle them.

## Design rules
- Landscape only, on every device (see Screens and orientation). New screens and HUD elements are designed for landscape and checked on small and large screens before they ship.
- No stamina system (too complex).
- No difficulty levels: one mode, calibrated to be fun, not impossible. Tuning happens through Settings.
- No speed ramps over time: speeds are fixed per state.
- No level numbers shown to the player: worlds are referred to by name only.
- Game tools (things you hold or can use) are grouped together, apart from the system buttons (sound, pause), but share their look and right-hand gutter on touch.
- Items you hold up to read are kept per world and picked from one place (the slot bar on PC, the item buttons on touch); adding a new kind of item never takes another item's place.
- Toasts are rare and terse; see Design principles and the list under Toasts.
- The map's arrow always shows the explorer's heading, never the camera's.
- Anything you hold up to read (a map, a note, future documents) is a held item: almost full screen with a thin border of world, proportions following the screen, its own background filling its shape, a close button (and zoom buttons if it zooms) in the top-right corner, and its own choice of which pads work, whether it zooms and whether it pauses the game.

## Technical rules
- Modular, multi-file code. Worlds are easy to add.
- Default tuning lives in `src/config.js`: `PLAYER` (running speed V and walking speed V/2), `GAME` (lives, gems needed per world in `quotas`), `GEMS` (`extraPerLevel`: gems beyond the quota, 0 so each world holds exactly the gems it needs; carrying cost; `humRadius` 14 m and `humRadiusBlind` 4 m for the proximity hum with and without a way to find gems), `PORTAL` (size, insertion, safe radius), `SPEED` (plain-chase hunters), `GLACIER` (the Yeti's speeds, approach distance, forget time, storms and the ice hole distance) and `AUDIO` (overall sound level at the menu and during a run). Hunter speeds are written as fractions of the running speed. Saved settings override these values at startup.
- **Proximity hum radius**: `main.js` uses `GEMS.humRadius` while the world allows the call (`canCall()`, or no hook) or `hasMap()` is true, and `GEMS.humRadiusBlind` otherwise. `g.near` (0–1) drives both the gem's glimmer and the gem hum voice.
- **Landscape only**: the `#rotate` notice in `index.html` is shown by CSS (`@media (orientation: portrait)`) above everything else. `main.js` watches the same media query (`PORTRAIT`): while it matches, `frame()` pauses a run in progress and returns before updating or rendering anything (menu included). The CSS and the code have no portrait layouts: responsive rules are written by screen height (e.g. `max-height: 500px` for phones held sideways, `max-aspect-ratio: 3/2` for the note's stacked layout, `max-height: 640px` for the menu summary) and width.
- **Responsive checks**: every build that changes something visible is screenshotted at the two development sizes listed under Screens and orientation (touch emulated for the phone) and checked for overlaps, clipping and unwanted scrolling.
- **Steering**: `src/engine/input.js` returns `getMove()` = `{ x, y, run }` (camera-relative) and accumulates camera drags in `consumeLook()`; `player.update(dt, move, rig.yaw, …)` moves and turns the explorer toward its walking direction; `rig.update(…)` orbits the camera. With the map open, `main.js` eases `player.heading` toward the camera's direction (`mapAlign`) once you turn the camera, until aligned.
- **Ground height = the drawn ground.** Each world shapes its terrain with a smooth height function (`smoothHeight`), then wraps it with `gridHeight(smoothHeight, TERRAIN_SIZE, quality.terrainSegments)` from `src/engine/terrain.js`. The result reads the same flat triangles the terrain mesh is built from, so the player, hunters, gems, props, storm edges and the safe-zone ring all stand exactly on the visible ground on every device (PC uses 200 terrain segments, phones 150). A new world must do the same, and must pass the same size and segment count to `createTerrain`.
- **Holes in the ground** (the Glacier's ice hole, `src/mechanics/iceCave.js`): the pit and cave are a fine heightfield of their own (0.2 m cells, so the ledge's edges are smooth), drawn from the same triangles it is read from. The ledge's shape is `pitLocal`: a flat landing at rim level for its first `LAND` radians (0.5, about 3.5 m along the rim), then a steady descent to the floor over the rest of `SWEEP`; the crown's gap and the marker pole are centred on the landing. The cave ceiling's shape is `ceilLocal` (base 3.3 m, tunnel vault +1 m, dome +2.4 m). `world.heightAt` reads it where the hole is and the terrain everywhere else (`world.groundAt` is the terrain alone); the terrain shader discards a circle over the opening. Cells too steep to walk block the explorer through `world.constrainMove`, which turns a blocked step by the smallest angle that works (keeping its forward part), so you glide along walls instead of stopping. `world.constrainCamera(camera, rig, pos, dt)` keeps the camera inside the hole and the cave: a cave weight rises smoothly from 2.5 m before the tunnel mouth to 1 m inside (and only near floor level), blending the camera to a lower, closer pose; where rock comes between you and the camera, the boom shortens quickly but smoothly and lengthens slowly, snapping in only if the smoothed spot would sit inside rock. `world.hunterKeepOut` lists circles hunters can't enter; `world.isSheltered(pos)` makes the hunter treat you as in a safe zone. Nothing underground casts shadows (the terrain doesn't either, so they'd fall on the snow above).
- **Dropping gems**: `main.js` `dropSpot(angle, dist)` picks where a gem is set down (Q, or when caught): it tries the given direction and distance, swings around, then comes closer, rejecting spots inside colliders, beyond `world.radius - 4` or more than 1 m above or below you; it falls back to your own spot.
- **Gems**: shapes are built once per style in `src/entities/gemDesigns.js` (`cluster`, `geode`, `rose`; each centred and sized to 1.6 m, with a 0.5 m mini version taken from the inner part when there is one). Parts have a role: `crystal` and `inner` glow and turn colour, `rock` only shades. A world module picks its gem with `gem: { style, dark, col, glow, inner?, innerDark?, glowK?, mapCol? }` (hidden colour, revealed colour, glow colour, the geode's lining colours, glow strength, map colour). `gemMapColor(look)` (mapCol, else inner, else col) colours the map's gem diamonds and, through the CSS variable `--gem` set at each level start, the map legend. `createGem(look)` and `createMiniGem(look)` in `src/entities/gem.js` take it; `main.js` passes `def.gem` to the gems, to `createPortal` (sockets) and to `player.setGemLook` (carried gems). The importmap also maps `three/addons/` (BufferGeometryUtils).
- Optional mechanics hooks read by `main.js` (documented in `src/worlds/registry.js`): `canCall()` (Resonance Call allowed), `introHint()`, `briefing(touch)`, `hasMap()`, `mapMarks()`, `onRespawn()` (its text is added to the catch toast), `action()` / `doAction(ctx)` (a contextual action within reach: `action()` returns `{ id, label, icon }` or null; it is shown as the action button in the tools group and used with Space or a tap; the Glacier uses it to take the map, and on success also calls `storms.shift`). The map is `src/ui/mapView.js`, drawn from `world.mapInfo`: the world is sampled once into a 256×256 grid (height, lake and cliff weights); the chart is drawn from it per pixel for the current view at the canvas's real resolution (CSS size × device pixel ratio, at most 2), with contour lines where the 3 m band changes between neighbouring pixels (lighter over cliff hatching), then trees, the playable edge and landmark labels as vectors. It is redrawn only when the view changes (zoom settles, resize, you move); while the zoom eases, the last drawing is stretched. Marks are drawn on top every frame in CSS px (`MARK`), with the arrow from `player.heading` and gems grouped into piles within 12 × `MARK` px. `layout()` sizes the chart (`--chart` on the sheet) on opening and on resize.
- **Held items** (`src/ui/held.js`): each item object has `open`, `hold = { pads: 'both' | 'right' | 'none', zoom, pauses }` and `zoomBy(f)`. `held.js` provides `Zoom` (a level easing toward its target) and `heldControls()` (the close button, plus zoom in / out buttons when given a `Zoom`; the only parts of an item that take clicks: the item itself has `pointer-events: none`, so drags reach the pads). The map and the note (`src/ui/briefing.js`) are `.held-item` elements inside `#hud`, inset by `--held-m`. `main.js` `heldItem()` returns the open one and `syncHolding()` (called whenever one opens or closes) sets `body.holding`, `hold-move` and `hold-look` (the CSS that hides the HUD and the pads that don't work) and `input.setHold({ move, look, zoom })`. With a hold set, `getMove()` returns nothing unless `move`, touches start the joystick only if `move` and the camera only if `look`, mouse drags look only if `look`, and two touches pinch and the wheel zooms only if `zoom`; `input.consumeZoom()` returns the zoom factor since the last frame, handed to the open item. With nothing held, the wheel steps through your items instead (`input.consumeWheel()`: one step per notch, or per 60 px of trackpad travel). An item with `pauses` puts the game in the `frozen` state (world drawn, music on, camera turning if allowed) until it closes. Keys: `ZOOM_KEYS` in `main.js` (= / + and − on the main row and keypad). `hud.attention(btn, on)` makes a tool glow until it's used (every newly found item).
- **Items** (`main.js`): `ITEMS` lists every kind of item: `{ id, btn, has(), isOpen(), open() }` (note: `briefing()` returns a page; map: `hasMap()`). `syncItems()` (each frame, and at level start) appends newly available ones to `items` in the order found, selects the newest and calls `hud.attention` on it, drops any the world no longer reports, and passes `items` and the selection to `hud.setItems(ids, sel)`. `items` is emptied at each level start. `selectItem(i)` (wheel, 1–9) wraps around; `useItem(id)` (Space on the selection, or a tap / click on the button) selects it and holds it up, or puts it away if it's already up; `closeHeld()` puts away the held item by hand and `putAway()` silently (caught, level change, menu). A new kind of item needs an `ITEMS` entry, a button with class `tool item` inside `#items` in `index.html` and an entry in `hud.itemBtns`. On touch, `hud.setItems` sets `--rows` on `#items` (min of the count and the CSS `--max-rows`) so the CSS grid fills a column, then wraps leftwards (`direction: rtl`).
- **HUD tools**: the tools group is `#tools` in `index.html` (the `#items` group with its `#item-label`, then the drop and action buttons); `hud.setItems`, `setDrop` and `setAction(action | null)` show and hide them, and `hud.fresh(btn)` makes a tool glow and show its name for a moment. Action icons live in the `ICONS` table in `src/ui/hud.js`. On touch the right-hand gutter is the CSS variable `--edge` on `#hud`, used by the tools column and the look pad (and matching `.hud-tr`).
- **Note page**: `briefing()` returns `{ image (SVG markup), text }` or null. While it returns a page, the note is one of your items (it calls for attention when first found, like every item); the Glacier returns it only once the note is taken (`src/mechanics/notePickup.js`: the paper in the snow, `canTake`, `take(ctx)`, `held`; the glacier's `action` hook offers it before the map). A world where the call is silent (`canCall()` false) skips the Resonance Call hint. The page is `src/ui/briefing.js` (a held item); the glacier's sketch is generated in `src/mechanics/iceCaveSketch.js` (deterministic; the crown's look matches the 3D hole, but the sketch shows no opening, gap or pole). The handwriting font is Caveat (Google Fonts, with cursive fallbacks).
- Walking through solid objects is prevented only by each world's colliders (circles and axis-aligned boxes added with the layout's `add`); anything without a collider can be walked through.
- The portal builds one socket per needed gem along its arch, so any quota works without touching the portal code.
- Hunter brains live in `src/hunters/`, world mechanics in `src/mechanics/`, the settings screen in `src/ui/settingsScreen.js`, the landing view in `src/ui/menuScene.js`, the menu/settings soundtrack in `src/ui/menuAudio.js`, the HUD in `src/ui/hud.js` (`setWorld(name)` sets the world label).
- The menu and the game share one camera. The menu frames it with zoom and a view offset, fitted to where the text ends, which `main.js` measures a few times a second and passes via `menu.setCopyRect(right)` (a fraction of the screen width); `menu.release(camera)` clears that framing before any world is rendered. Any decoration added behind the menu text must stay inside the screen, or the menu would become scrollable (the menu screen has overflow hidden as a safeguard).
- Audio is fully procedural (Web Audio). Continuous in-game voices are driven by the per-frame update; `audio.quiet()` silences them whenever that update stops running (the menu).
- **Loudness**: every sound bus (effects, ambient, music) goes through one shared level stage, set with `audio.setLevel()` and smoothed: `AUDIO.menuLevel` (1) at the menu and Settings, `AUDIO.runLevel` (0.4) from the start of each level. The menu track is written quiet while the worlds' sounds are written louder, so the run level brings them close together without touching each world's own mix. If the game still sounds louder than the menu, lower `runLevel`; if too faint, raise it. The mute button works separately on the master gain.
- The safe zone is enforced for every hunter in `src/entities/monster.js`: it tells the brain `ctx.safe` (plus `ctx.portal`), keeps the hunter out of the zone and switches its danger cues off. `ctx.safe` is also true while `world.isSheltered` says you're hidden. A new brain only has to decide what to do while `ctx.safe` is true. The ring is drawn by `src/entities/portal.js` (only when the world's `heightAt` is passed in) and rebuilt at each level start if the radius changed.
- A world mechanic can react to gem deliveries through an optional `onGemInserted(ctx)` hook (see `src/worlds/registry.js`). In the glacier, `src/mechanics/snowStorms.js` exposes `shift(ctx, { soundAt, toastAt })` (moves every device, plays the chime and shows "Winds are changing…"), used by `onGemInserted` (unless a shift happened in the last 10 s) and when the map is taken. The glacier's mechanics update calls `storms.calm()` whenever `cave.sheltered(player)` is true and keeps storms disabled there.
- The `portal-runner` GitHub repo is the single source of truth for the code and these notes. Plain static files with no build step (Three.js 0.160 comes from the jsDelivr CDN through the importmap in `index.html`). It runs from any static server (ES modules don't load from `file://`); see `CLAUDE.md` for how to run it on PC and on a phone.
- Every build is tested on mobile and must also work on PC.
